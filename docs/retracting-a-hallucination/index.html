07 / why this is not an apology

The difference is what the next request contains

An apology is another message. It sits underneath the mistake, and the mistake goes out again on the next request, and the one after that, for as long as the conversation lasts. The model is then in the position of having to out-argue its own transcript on every turn, and models are not good at that. They tend to defer to what is written above them, because that is what they were trained to do.

Here the invention is not underneath anything. It is gone from the messages, because the items it lived on were rewritten, and the request is built from the items. From request sixteen onwards this model is answering a conversation in which it never said any of it.

Nothing was destroyed to get there. Replacing an item's content is the one event in this runtime that carries the content itself, precisely because overwritten text is the only thing nothing else can recover:

08 / the honest part

Caveats, before you get excited

  • It was told. The model did not notice on its own. What is its own is everything after the instruction: which two of its sixteen items were the wrong ones, what to put there instead, and the account of why it went wrong. Nobody said "item 10 and item 14".
  • The tools were on, and the person said yes. amend is off by default and every call it made was approved at the prompt, the same way the two it made to look at the disk were refused. A model that can rewrite its own context is a decision, not a default.
  • Some of the cleanup is just tidying. Twenty-one of the items it excluded are its own tool calls and their results. That is housekeeping, and it is not the interesting part; the two revisions are.
  • One model, one run. This is gemini-3.6-flash, through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, in one conversation. It is a recording of what happened once, not a claim about what always happens.
  • It still cannot tell you what nachalnik is. The correction is a retraction, not knowledge. It ends by asking to be told, which is the right answer and was available from the first turn.

Runtime: nachalnik, an agent runtime in which the context, the tools, the permissions and the requests are explicit state you can read, change and put back. The two tools used here, introspect and amend, are a few hundred lines of ordinary user code against its public API.
